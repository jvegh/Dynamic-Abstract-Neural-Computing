2.1 Cell pressure

The electrolyte contains dissociated molecules (free charges), which, due to the repulsion between the ions, are located on the surfaces of the membrane and exert a pressure on the membrane surface. We assume that the relationship between the amount of the charge on the membrane and the pressure is linear, i.e., we conclude a pressure change from a charge change, and extrapolate it to the neuron in the resting state.

Physics panel 2.1.1:
Estimating the force exerted on ions in electric field   By using that in an electric field F→=q⁢E→, one can calculate the force exerted on a charge, that enables one to calculate also the particle’s impulse, pressure, and energy. For example, in the case of N⁢a+ rush-in, the electric force field is 107 [V/m]. Since([N/C]==[V/m]), the value of the force exerted on a single ion, accelerating it across the ion channel, is FN⁢a+=107⁢[N/C]×1.602∗10−19⁢[C]=1.6∗10−12⁢[N] (2.1) (Here we used data from Johnston&Wu constants from Numerics panel 2.1)

Physics panel 2.1.2:
Estimating the acceleration of ions in electric field   The force in the ion channel was calculated in Physics Panel 2.1 and the mass of a N⁢a+ ion is mN⁢a=3.82×10−26⁢k⁢g. It causes an acceleration a=FN⁢a+mN⁢a+=1.6∗10−12⁢[N]3.82×10−26⁢[k⁢g]=0.42×1014⁢[ms2] (2.2) The distance d to travel (without collision) is the thickness of the neuronal membrane 5∗10−9⁢n⁢m and we assume a continuosly accelerating ion. Using d=12×a×t2 tt⁢r⁢a⁢v⁢e⁢r⁢s⁢e=2×5∗10−9⁢[m]0.42×1014⁢[ms2]=1.54×10−11⁢[s] (2.3) The acceleration of an ion is unbelievably large. It is sufficiently large to keep the potential at the same value over an extended object, provided that the ions must follow a small and slow change, such as due to the ”leaking” current through the Axon Initial Segment (AIS). Similarly, the ions can ’instantly’ follow quick changes such as a square wave gradient.

Physics panel 2.1.3:
Estimating the speed of ions in electric field   In Physics Panel 2.2, we estimated the acceleration and passage time of a N⁢a+ ion during a rush-in action. In this period the ion is accelerated to the speed v=a∗t vN⁢a+=0.42×1014⁢[ms2]× 1.54×10−11⁢[s]=600⁢[ms] (2.4) (This speed is just a speed component along the channel. The thermal speed is about an order of magnitude higher, but not necessarily in the direction of the channel. The speed due to acceleration may convert in direction and magnitude due to a single collision, as soon as the diaphragm effect of the channel is over. The accelerated ion ’stops’ as soon as the accelerating voltage is over.) See our other estimation in Physics Panel 2.1.

Estimating the current of ion channels   Based on the time caculated in Physics Panel 2.2, we can attribute to an ion channel for this short period, provided that 103 ions pass through a single ion channel, a current Ic⁢h⁢a⁢n⁢n⁢e⁢l=103×1.60×10−19⁢[C]1.54×10−11⁢[s]=10−5⁢A (2.5) It is in the range of several μ⁢As, for a short period. It is distributed in the spatiotemporal surface of a neuron, and contributes to a current of dozen of p⁢As.)

Physics panel 2.1.5:
Estimating the pressure change due to rushin   Using the force from Physics panel 2.1, one can estimate how the pressure of the neural cell increases due to the rush-in changes at the beginning of the Action Potential (AP). As evidence shows, the local potential at the internal surface of the membrane is in the range of 100 mV in the resting state and increases by Δ⁢U= 100 mV in the transition state. This increase means a change in the force acting on an ion (see Eq. (2.1)) by 1.6∗10−12⁢[N]. When we assume 107 rush-in ions and unchanged cell size, the total force acting on the membrane increases by 1.6∗10−5⁢[N]. This change in force means on the neuron’s 8∗10−9⁢[m2] surface a pressure change Δ⁢P=1.6∗10−5⁢[N]8∗10−9⁢[m2]=2∗103[Nm2] (2.6) Again, the pressure can be calculated using electricity. (Here we used data from Johnston&Wu constants from Numerics panel 2.1)

Physics panel 2.1.6:
Estimating the force measurable due to rushin   The pressure change calculated in Physics panel 2.6 enables to calculate the force measurable dur to rushin. That pressure on a 10×10⁢μ⁢m tooltip of the measuring device is converted to a force 2∗103∗100∗10−12=200⁢p⁢N force. The measured force value [43] is about 600⁢p⁢N, so our estimation is in the correct range. The measured pressure value is [PressureChangeActionPotentialMeasured:1980] 5⁢d⁢y⁢nc⁢m2=0.5⁢Nm2, which is an average value for some period. If we assume a 1⁢H⁢z frequency, and a 1⁢m⁢s period for the duration of the AP peak, it means 1∗103⁢[Nm2]. So, the measured mechanical change values [NeuronalDeformation:2020] underpin our hypothesis that the increased pressure increases the neuron’s size via its elasticity.

Physics panel 2.1.7:
Estimating the rush-in speed   A way to estimate the ions’ rush-in speed is that one assumes that the Stokes-Einstein speed describes the ions’ speed (Fig. 3 in [5], quantitatively underpins the hypothesis, see [VeghStokesEinstein:2025]) both in axons and ion channels. If so, the ratio of the potential gradients equals the ratio of the speeds of ions. One can estimate that the 100⁢m⁢V AP on the 50⁢μ⁢m AIS generates a 2∗104⁢Vm electrical gradient, and [5] measured 20⁢ms for the speed of the AP. By assuming that the electrical field across the membrane is 107⁢Vm, one can estimate the speed at the exit of the ion channels as 104⁢ms. See our other estimation in Physics Panel 2.4

Physics panel 2.1.8:
Estimating the rush-in mechanical work   By using the result derived in Physics panel 2.6, one can also estimate the work done when the rush-in charge extends the neuron, as the Δ⁢P×Δ⁢V. We consider that Δ⁢P remains constant, and we calculate the change of the volume as neuron’s surface 10−8⁢m2 multiplied by the change of the radius Δ⁢r=10−9⁢[m] multiplied by the pressure Δ⁢P, that gives Em⁢e⁢c⁢h=2∗10−14⁢[J]. If one assumes that the pressure is of entirely mechanical origin [NeuronalDeformation:2020], one must assume that the pressure is 106⁢Nm2, which is well above the measured value.

Physics panel 2.1.10:
Estimating the rush-in time period   To estimate the time period required to change the pressure calculated in Physics panel 2.6, one can assume that the collision force of a N⁢a+ ion provides the pressure (the shock wave), and this can be calculated as F=m⁢Δ⁢vΔ⁢t.
