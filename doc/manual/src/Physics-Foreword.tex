\index{ordinary laws}


The existence of life is still a mystery for science. 
Why Schr\"odinger desperately inserted the boldfaced word was his firm conviction that the "different construction" the living matter represents only needs different (cross-disciplinary) approximations, and they result in non-ordinary laws, which, "once they have been revealed,
will form just as integral a part of this science as the former".~\cite{Schrodinger:1992}
Eight decades later, E. Schrödinger's question is still open. "The origins of life stands among the great open scientific questions of our time"~\cite{OriginsOfLifeWalker:2017}. We hope the analyzis in this chapter takes us one step closer to the reply.
We prepare the concepts and principles which enable us to
answer E. Schrödinger's question concerning the role of physics in describing life.


Our \hypertarget{NeuronPhysical}{abstract model} focuses on the
neuron's signal processing functionality. 
In this chapter, we review physical terms, concepts and laws,
from the point of view of biology.
We scrutinize the oversimplifications, wrong abstractions and derive the laws
which are not unknown but need a more careful formulation of the first principles when applied to living matter.
"We consider
how transient electrical signals are generated in the neuron." ~\cite{PrinciplesNeuralScience:2013}, page 126.
We note, however, that the electrical phenomena are closely related to other disciplines, mainly thermodynamics.
More precisely, neither of the classical disciplines
can describe the electrolyte-related phenomena alone.
Our specific goal is to establish the firm base of a cross-disciplinary model of neuronal operation.
We are as general as possible when discussing the
physics of ions and electrolytes; furthermore, the physics for biology. Typically, the discusion of features in this chapter is interrupted (it prepares the physiological discussion) and continued in the next chapter.
